# Cambodia Travel Destinations — Research Report for GoKH

Nov 21, 2026 · @Hi

## Overview

Cambodia has 20 destinations in five regions that are worth putting in GoKH. Each one is covered below with what to see, how long to stay, how to get there and what it costs. Angkor (Siem Reap) and Phnom Penh are the core of almost every trip. The south coast, Battambang, the old temple cities and the wild northeast are good add-ons for longer trips.

This report is written as source material for [GoKH](https://gokh.vercel.app), a trip planner for exploring Cambodia together. Section 8 has a table of destinations with fields that can go straight into the app (region, days, best for, getting there).

**Cambodia at a glance**

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Capital | Phnom Penh |
| Language | Khmer (English widely used in tourist areas) |
| Currency | Cambodian riel (KHR); US dollars accepted almost everywhere. Change under $1 is usually given in riel |
| Time zone | UTC+7 (same as Bangkok) |
| Main airports | Techo International (KTI, Phnom Penh), Siem Reap-Angkor International (SAI), Sihanouk International (KOS) |

**The five regions in this report**

1. Siem Reap & Angkor: the temples, Kulen and Tonle Sap lake
2. Phnom Penh: the capital, its palace, museums and history
3. South coast & islands: Kampot, Kep, Sihanoukville, Koh Rong and Koh Rong Sanloem
4. Northwest & heritage sites: Battambang, Koh Ker, Beng Mealea, Sambor Prei Kuk, Preah Vihear
5. Nature & northeast: Kratie, Mondulkiri, Ratanakiri, the Cardamom Mountains and Koh Kong

## Practical travel essentials

Most visitors need a $30 tourist visa and a free e-Arrival card, and should fly in rather than cross from Thailand. Land crossings with Thailand are closed for now because of the border conflict (see Safety below).

**Visa and entry**

- **Tourist e-visa (Type T):** $30 at the official portal [evisa.gov.kh](https://www.evisa.gov.kh). It gives one entry and a 30-day stay, and must be used within 3 months of issue ([Issa Compass](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/cambodia-tourist-and-business-e-visa-2026-fees-border-closures-and-who-travels-v)). The old $6 processing fee was dropped on 1 January 2025 ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/kh/pdf/technical-update/2025/tu-cambodia-e-visa-fee-reductions-and-streamlined-process-en.pdf)).
- **Visa on arrival:** also available at the airports for a 30-day stay. Bring clean US dollar notes.
- **Visas are now digital:** Cambodia stopped issuing paper visa stickers on 3 October 2026 ([Air Traveler Club](https://www.airtraveler.club/intel/cambodia-e-visa-cost-2026-36-all-in-and-no-passport-sticker/)).
- **e-Arrival card (CeA):** every air traveller must submit it, whatever their nationality or visa. It is free on the official portal or app; sites that charge are not official ([Immigration World](https://www.immigrationworld.com/cambodia/cambodia-tourist-visa/)). Submit it in the 7 days before the flight.
- **Extensions:** a tourist visa can be extended once, for 30 more days, at the Immigration Department in Phnom Penh.
- **ASEAN citizens** (including Thai visitors) can enter visa-free.

**Airports**

| Airport | Code | Serves | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Techo International](https://www.techoairport.com.kh/news/techo-intl-airport-welcome-first-flight-09-sep-2025) | KTI | Phnom Penh | Opened 9 September 2025, about 20 km outside the city. It replaced the old Phnom Penh airport (PHN), which closed the same day ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/travel/cambodia-s-largest-airport-begins-operations-4936965.html)) |

| Sihanouk International | KOS | Sihanoukville, the islands, Kampot | Mostly regional flights |

**When to go**

| Season | Months | What it is like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cool and dry (peak) | November to February | The most popular time, with milder heat ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/best-time-to-visit-cambodia)). Busiest at Angkor |
| Hot and dry | March to May | The hottest months, often in the low-to-mid 30s °C |
| Wet (green season) | June to October | Usually one heavy shower a day, green rice fields and hotel discounts. September is the wettest month |

The wet season is the best time for Tonle Sap floating villages and waterfalls. The dry season is better for the islands, trekking and the Cardamom Mountains.

**Money and getting around**

- US dollars are used for most tourist prices; riel is used for small change and local markets. Bakong / ABA QR payments are widely accepted.
- In cities, the ride-hailing apps PassApp and Grab are the easiest way to book tuk-tuks and cars at fixed prices.
- Between cities, the main options are intercity buses and minivans, private taxis, domestic flights between Phnom Penh and Siem Reap, and the Royal Railway train from Phnom Penh to Kampot and Sihanoukville. The Phnom Penh to Sihanoukville expressway has cut that drive to a few hours.

**Safety**

Most of Cambodia is safe for travel, but the Thai border area is not. Thailand and Cambodia fought along the border in 2025 and signed a ceasefire on 27 December 2025, but the situation is still tense.

- Canada says to avoid all travel within 50 km of the Thai border, and notes that land border crossings with Thailand are closed ([Government of Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/Cambodia.html), updated 14 October 2026).
- The UK advises against all but essential travel within 20 km of the border. It lists Preah Vihear temple as closed ([UK FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/cambodia/local-laws-and-customs)).
- The US rates Cambodia Level 2 (increased caution, for crime and landmines) and Level 4 (do not travel) for the border area ([US State Department](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/cambodia-travel-advisory.html)).
- Landmines and unexploded ordnance remain in some rural areas. Stay on marked paths and use a local guide in forests and dry rice fields.
- In cities, the main risk is petty crime such as bag snatching from motorbikes.

## 1. Siem Reap & Angkor

Siem Reap is the gateway to Angkor, Cambodia's top attraction, and most travellers spend 3 to 4 days here. The Angkor Archaeological Park holds hundreds of Khmer temples built between the 9th and 15th centuries.

**Angkor Pass (official prices, 2026)**

| Pass | Price | Valid for | Photo needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-day | $37 | The date on the ticket | No |
| 3-day | $62 | Any 3 days within 7 days | Yes |
| 7-day | $72 | Any 7 days within 30 days | Yes |

Source: [Adventures Cambodia](https://adventurescambodia.com/blog/angkor-wat-tickets/), checked against the official site in August 2026. Buy only from [Angkor Enterprise](https://angkorenterprise.gov.kh) online or at the ticket office on Road 60. Children under 12 enter free. A ticket dated for tomorrow lets you in for sunset from 4:45 pm the evening before. The pass also covers Banteay Srei, Kbal Spean, Beng Mealea, Phnom Krom and the Roluos group, but **not** Koh Ker or Phnom Kulen.

**Must-see temples**

| Site | Why go | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Angkor Wat | The world's largest religious monument, and the symbol on Cambodia's flag | Arrive before 5:30 am for sunrise over the reflecting pools |
| Angkor Thom & Bayon | Walled royal city; the Bayon has more than 200 giant stone faces | Go mid-morning after the sunrise crowd leaves |
| Ta Prohm | The "jungle temple", with tree roots growing over the ruins | Very busy; go early or late |
| Banteay Srei | Small 10th-century temple with the finest pink sandstone carving | About 1 hour north of town; pair it with Kbal Spean |
| Preah Khan, Neak Pean, Ta Som | The "Grand Circuit": quieter temples spread over a wider loop | Best by tuk-tuk or bicycle on day 2 |
| Roluos Group | The earliest Angkor-era capital (late 9th century) | Short half-day trip from town |

**Beyond the temples**

- **Phnom Kulen National Park:** a sacred mountain about 50 km from Angkor Wat, with waterfalls you can swim in, the River of a Thousand Lingas and a giant reclining Buddha. Foreigners pay $20 per day, in cash ([Kulen Revealed](https://kulenrevealed.org/what-is-the-phnom-kulen-national-park-entrance-fee)). The road up is only open from 7 to 11 am, so leave Siem Reap by 9 am.
- **Tonle Sap floating villages:** Kampong Phluk, Kampong Khleang and Chong Kneas are stilt and floating villages on Southeast Asia's largest lake. Visit in the wet season; water is low in the dry months.
- **In town:** Pub Street and the night markets, the Angkor National Museum, Phare (the Cambodian circus), Khmer cooking classes, and rural bike or quad-bike tours.

**Suggested length:** 3 days (Small Circuit, Grand Circuit, then Banteay Srei or Kulen). Add a 4th day for Beng Mealea and Koh Ker (section 4).

## 2. Phnom Penh

Phnom Penh, the capital, needs 2 to 3 days and mixes royal sights, a lively riverfront and the memorials of the Khmer Rouge years. The city sits where the Tonle Sap and Mekong rivers meet.

| Site | What it is | Entry (foreign adult) | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Royal Palace & Silver Pagoda | Home of the King; the Silver Pagoda floor has more than 5,000 silver tiles | About $10 ([Hello Angkor](https://helloangkor.com/?p=7296)) | Daily, 8–11 am and 2–5 pm; closes for royal ceremonies |
| National Museum of Cambodia | The world's largest collection of Khmer sculpture, next to the palace | Small fee | Daily |
| [Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (S-21)](https://tuolsleng.gov.kh/en/) | A school turned into a Khmer Rouge prison | $5, plus $5 for the audio guide ([GoAsia](https://goasia.cc/en/article/tuol-sleng-museum)) | Daily, 8 am–5 pm; cash only |
| Choeung Ek Genocidal Center | The "Killing Fields" memorial, about 15 km south of the city | $3 entry plus $3 audio guide (about $6 total) ([Pelago](https://pelago.com/en-AU/activity/ptd5k-tuol-sleng-museum-choeung-ek-memorial-phnom-penh)) | Daily |
| Wat Phnom | The hilltop temple the city is named after | Small fee | Daily |
| Sisowath Quay (riverside) | Promenade, cafés and sunset river cruises | Free; cruises from a few dollars | Best at sunset |
| Central Market & Russian Market | Art-deco domed market and the Tuol Tom Poung market for crafts and souvenirs | Free | Mornings are coolest |
| Koh Dach (Silk Island) | Mekong island of silk-weaving villages | Ferry or tour | Half day |

Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek are part of the UNESCO-listed Cambodian Memorial Sites. They are usually visited together in a half-day. The audio guides are strongly recommended, and visitors should dress modestly and keep quiet. In the app, these sites could carry a short content note, since they are not suitable for young children.

**Getting there:** flights land at Techo International (KTI). Buses and minivans reach Siem Reap in about 6 hours, Kampot in about 3 hours, and Sihanoukville in about 3 hours via the expressway.

## 3. South coast & islands

The south coast is where travellers slow down after the temples: riverside Kampot, seaside Kep, and white-sand islands reached from Sihanoukville. Plan 3 to 6 days for this region.

**Kampot**

Kampot is a quiet riverside town of French colonial shophouses below Bokor Mountain, 3 to 4 hours from Phnom Penh ([Hostelworld](https://www.hostelworld.com/blog/?p=1305412)). Most visitors stay about 3 days.

- **Kampot pepper farms:** Kampot pepper has protected geographical indication (GI) status in the EU ([Travel Dojo](https://www.traveldojo.com/cambodia/kampot)). La Plantation and other farms offer free tours and tastings.
- **Bokor National Park:** a misty plateau with the ruins of a French hill station, waterfalls and the Lok Yeay Mao statue. Bring a jacket; it is much cooler on top.
- **Also:** sunset river cruises, the salt fields, limestone caves near Kampong Trach, and the night market.

**Kep**

Kep is a small seaside town about 30 minutes from Kampot. It is famous for its [Crab Market](https://www.nomads-travel-guide.com/city/kep), where fresh crab is cooked with green Kampot pepper. Boats leave for Koh Tonsay (Rabbit Island), and Kep National Park has short trails with sea views.

**Sihanoukville and the islands**

Sihanoukville is mainly a transit point today: its port and the Sihanouk International Airport (KOS) are the gateway to the islands.

| Island | Character | Ferry from Sihanoukville |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Koh Rong | Larger and livelier; Koh Touch village, long empty beaches like Long Set and Sok San, jungle treks, plankton swims at night | About 45 to 90 minutes by speed ferry |
| Koh Rong Sanloem | Quieter and family-friendly; Saracen Bay, Lazy Beach and M'Pai Bay; snorkelling and diving | About 40 to 90 minutes by speed ferry ([Ferryscanner](https://www.ferryscanner.com/en/ferry-routes/ferry-sihanoukville-all-ports-koh-rong-sanloem-all-ports)) |

Ferries are run by several companies and leave from different piers, so the app should show the pier with each booking. Sea conditions are best from November to May.

**Getting there:** Kampot and Sihanoukville are about 3 hours from Phnom Penh by bus, minivan or expressway taxi. The Royal Railway also runs a scenic train from Phnom Penh to Kampot and Sihanoukville.

## 4. Northwest & heritage sites

Battambang and the remote temple cities give travellers Khmer history without the Angkor crowds. Preah Vihear temple is closed for now because it sits on the Thai border.

| Destination | What it is | From | Entry | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Battambang | Cambodia's second city, with colonial shophouses and a strong art scene | 3 h from Siem Reap ([Hostelworld](https://www.hostelworld.com/blog/?p=1305411)) | Free | Open |
| Beng Mealea | A huge Angkor Wat-era temple, mostly unrestored and swallowed by jungle | About 40 km east of the main Angkor group | Included in the Angkor Pass, or a single ticket | Open |
| [Koh Ker](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Koh_Ker) | 10th-century royal capital with a 7-tier pyramid temple (Prasat Thom); UNESCO World Heritage since 2023 | About 120 km northeast of Siem Reap | $15, not covered by the Angkor Pass ([Travel Cost Calculator](https://travelcostcalculator.org/unesco/koh-ker-archaeological-site-of-ancient-lingapura-or-chok-gargyar)) | Open |
| [Sambor Prei Kuk](https://helloangkor.com/attractions/sambor-prei-kuk-group-visiting-guide-history-more/) | 7th-century brick temples of the pre-Angkor capital Ishanapura; UNESCO World Heritage since 2017 | 30 km north of Kampong Thom town, on the Phnom Penh–Siem Reap road | Small fee; cash only | Open |
| Preah Vihear | Cliff-top temple on the Dangrek escarpment; UNESCO World Heritage | Northern border | — | Closed (border area, see Safety) |

**Battambang highlights**

- **Bamboo train (norry):** a bamboo platform on wheels powered by a small motor, now on a short tourist track. The ride costs about $5 ([Gecko Routes](https://www.geckoroutes.com/?p=4533323)).
- **Phnom Sampov:** a hilltop temple, the Killing Caves memorial, and the Bat Cave, where millions of bats stream out at sunset.
- **Phare Ponleu Selpak:** the arts and circus school founded in 1994, with evening circus shows.
- **Also:** Wat Banan hilltop temple, the colonial old town and Street 2.5, Wat Ko old wooden houses, and countryside cycling.

**How to visit:** Koh Ker and Beng Mealea fit into one long day trip from Siem Reap. Sambor Prei Kuk is a good stop when driving between Phnom Penh and Siem Reap. Battambang needs 2 nights. Because of the border situation, GoKH should check the latest advice before suggesting any site close to the Thai border.

## 5. Nature & northeast

The northeast and the Cardamom Mountains are Cambodia's wild side: river dolphins, elephant sanctuaries, crater lakes and rainforest. They suit travellers with 2 weeks or more, or people coming back for a second trip.

**Kratie: Mekong river dolphins**

The critically endangered Irrawaddy dolphins live in deep pools of the Mekong in Kratie and Stung Treng provinces. The Kampi pool, near Kratie town, is the easiest place to see them by small boat ([Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/09/mekongs-irrawaddy-dolphins-find-cautious-hope-amid-a-river-in-crisis/)). The population is slowly rising: a calf spotted in April 2026 brought the count to 118 ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/04/28/latest-kratie-dolphin-calf-sighting-brings-population-in-cambodia-to-118)). The dry season is best, when dolphins stay in the deep pools. Kratie also has Koh Trong island for cycling and riverside sunsets.

**Mondulkiri (Sen Monorom)**

Mondulkiri is a hilly, forested province, home to the Bunong people. Its main draw is ethical elephant sanctuaries where visitors walk with rescued elephants instead of riding them:

- **[Elephant Valley Project](https://grassrootsvolunteering.org/businesses/elephant-valley-project/):** 11 km from Sen Monorom; day visits with transport and lunch from about $55 to $85.
- **The Mondulkiri Project:** run by the Cambodian Elephant Rescue Organisation, with forest treks, waterfalls and swimming holes.
- **Also:** Bou Sra waterfall, grassland hills and coffee farms.

**Ratanakiri (Banlung)**

The far northeast, known for red-earth roads and highland forest. The highlight is **Yeak Laom**, a round, clear crater lake for swimming, run as a protected area by the local indigenous community. Visitors also go to waterfalls (Ka Tieng, Cha Ong) and on river and jungle treks into Virachey National Park.

**Cardamom Mountains & Koh Kong**

The Cardamoms in the southwest hold one of the largest rainforests left in mainland Southeast Asia.

- **[Chi Phat](https://tourismcambodia.com/activities/community-based-tourism-cbt/chi-phat-community-based-ecotourism-site.htm):** a community-based ecotourism village set up in 2007 with the NGO Wildlife Alliance. Locals run homestays, jungle treks, cycling and river trips.
- **Tatai River:** eco-lodges, Tatai waterfall and mangrove kayaking.
- **Note:** Koh Kong town sits on the Thai border, inside the area some governments advise against. Check the latest advice before booking.

**Getting there:** these areas are reached by road. Kratie is about 4 to 5 hours from Phnom Penh. Sen Monorom and Banlung are a long day's drive. The dry season (November to May) has the best road conditions.

## Sample itineraries and destination data

Three route templates cover most GoKH users: a 7-day classic, a 10-day temples-and-coast trip, and a 14-day trip that adds the wild northeast.

**7 days: Classic Cambodia**

1. Arrive Phnom Penh (KTI): riverside walk, sunset cruise
2. Phnom Penh: Royal Palace, National Museum, Tuol Sleng, Choeung Ek
3. Fly or bus to Siem Reap; evening at Pub Street and the night market
4. Angkor: Angkor Wat sunrise, Angkor Thom and Bayon, Ta Prohm
5. Angkor: Grand Circuit (Preah Khan, Neak Pean, Ta Som)
6. Banteay Srei and Kbal Spean, or Phnom Kulen; evening Phare circus
7. Tonle Sap floating village (wet season) or Roluos Group; fly out (SAI)

**10 days: Temples & coast**

1. Days 1–4: Siem Reap and Angkor (as above), with a Koh Ker and Beng Mealea day trip
2. Day 5: Drive to Phnom Penh, stopping at Sambor Prei Kuk
3. Day 6: Phnom Penh sights
4. Days 7–8: Kampot and Kep (pepper farm, Bokor, crab market)
5. Days 9–10: Koh Rong Sanloem, then back to Phnom Penh or fly out from Sihanoukville (KOS)

**14 days: Grand tour**

1. Days 1–4: Siem Reap and Angkor
2. Days 5–6: Battambang (bamboo train, Bat Cave, circus)
3. Days 7–8: Phnom Penh
4. Days 9–10: Kratie dolphins, then Mondulkiri elephant sanctuary
5. Days 11–14: Kampot, Kep and the islands

## Responsible travel and notes for GoKH

GoKH can make trips better for travellers and local people by building a few etiquette reminders and safety flags into each destination page.

**Etiquette to show in the app**

- **Dress code at temples and the Royal Palace:** cover shoulders and knees. Remove shoes and hats where asked.
- **Monks:** women should not touch monks or hand things to them directly. Ask before taking photos of people.
- **Body language:** don't touch anyone's head or point your feet at people or Buddha images.
- **Memorial sites:** keep quiet and dress modestly at Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek.
- **Animals:** choose sanctuaries where visitors walk with elephants, not ride them.
- **Children:** don't give money to child sellers, and avoid orphanage tours. Supporting family-run businesses and social enterprises helps more.
- **Landmines:** stay on marked paths in rural and forest areas.

**Suggested product features**

1. **Status badge per destination** (Open / Check advice / Closed), linked to the latest government advice, so groups don't plan around closed sites such as Preah Vihear.
2. **Entry-fee field** for each site, with a "last checked" date. Angkor Pass, Kulen and Koh Ker fees are the ones travellers ask about most.
3. **Season filter:** a dry-season / wet-season toggle that reorders suggestions (islands and trekking in the dry season, Tonle Sap and waterfalls in the wet season).
4. **Pre-trip checklist** shared with the whole group: e-visa, e-Arrival card, cash in clean US dollars, and modest clothing for temples.
5. **Group cost split** for shared tuk-tuks, boats and drivers, since these are usually paid per vehicle, not per person.
6. **Ride-hailing deep links** to PassApp and Grab for city transfers.
